## What the numbers changed

On the 4 cross-page questions, baseline dense-only page recall was **1.000**, hybrid+rerank alone (decomposition and graph expansion OFF) raised it to **1.000**, and enabling query decomposition + graph expansion raised it further to **1.000**. See ADR.md 'What we changed because of the eval numbers' for the concrete decision this drove (the `RERANK_SCORE_FLOOR` / decomposition-gate tuning) and the exact before/after run that justified it.

## Failure case analysis

### `attn_abstract_vs_cost_cross_page` (cross_page)
**Q:** According to the abstract, what BLEU score did the model achieve on English-to-German, and what does the results section say made that result notable given the training cost?
- Full-pipeline page recall: 1.000 · retrieved pages: [('attention_is_all_you_need.pdf', 8), ('attention_is_all_you_need.pdf', 8), ('attention_is_all_you_need.pdf', 8), ('scanned_attention_excerpt.pdf', 1), ('attention_is_all_you_need.pdf', 1)]
- Expected: [{'document': 'attention_is_all_you_need.pdf', 'pages': [1, 8]}]
- Refused: True (correct: False)
- Judge: faithfulness=0.9, relevance=0.8 - The answer correctly identifies the BLEU score of 28.4 on English-to-German from the abstract/text. However, it claims the documents do not contain enough information about the English-to-German training cost, whereas Source 1 explicitly states that even the base model surpasses previous models at a fraction of the training cost, and Source 1/2 provide specific details about the big transformer's training cost and relative efficiency.
